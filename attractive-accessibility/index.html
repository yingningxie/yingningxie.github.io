Attractive accessibility

Exploring disparities in attributes of primary care physicians in New Jersey.

Research companion · Health & Place · 2025

Which doctors can you reach in 30 minutes?

Finding a doctor involves more than reaching a nearby clinic. A physician’s qualifications, patient ratings, and languages spoken can shape whether that provider meets a patient’s needs.

In this study, we examine access to primary care in New Jersey by car, public transit, and e-bike. We introduce attractive accessibility: access to providers with attributes that patients may value.

Transport options shape the providers within reach

Cars provide the highest average access for every provider group. E-bikes offer greater average access than transit, including to board-certified, highly rated, and multilingual physicians. Their potential varies with cycling infrastructure and a person’s ability to ride.

03 / Disparities within reach

The mix of accessible doctors matters

We compare both the level of access to providers with specific attributes and its ratio to access to all providers. This ratio describes the share of the weighted accessible physician pool with a given attribute.

Greater overall access

For car and transit, census tracts with higher concentrations of socially disadvantaged populations generally have greater spatial access to primary care physicians, including providers with the attributes studied.

A smaller highly rated share

Across all three modes, greater social vulnerability is associated with a lower proportion of highly rated physicians among accessible providers.

Language access aligns differently

Tracts with more residents with limited English proficiency tend to have access to a higher proportion of Spanish-speaking physicians, with stronger associations for car and e-bike than for transit.

04 / Spatial patterns

Where access changes

Providers cluster around New York City and Philadelphia. Accounting for the population they serve changes the picture: a large number of nearby doctors can still mean moderate access when competition is high.

Connections across the Hudson matter

In some tracts near New York City, restrictions on cycling across the Hudson limit e-bike access to physicians. Transit can provide greater access than e-bikes in these locations, despite its lower statewide average.

05 / Planning implications

Expand the choices within reach

Improving transit service and cycling connections could expand the range of primary care providers available to households without cars, including physicians with particular qualifications or language skills. The findings support considering provider attributes alongside the number of doctors within reach. Further research could examine how patients’ time constraints, clinic opening hours, and insurance acceptance shape the choices they can use.

06 / About the study

Measuring access to providers people value

We combine Healthgrades physician profiles, road and transit networks, census data, and the CDC Social Vulnerability Index. The analysis covers family practice and internal medicine physicians in New Jersey and neighboring parts of New York, Pennsylvania, and Delaware.

The two-step floating catchment area method first estimates each provider’s population catchment using a 30-minute drive. It then sums provider-to-population ratios for physicians reachable from each tract by the selected travel mode. Using the same driving-based population catchments across modes accounts for competition from the broader population a provider serves.

Transit estimates use scheduled service between 10 and 11 a.m. on September 9, 2024. E-bike travel is modeled at up to 20 mph with network restrictions. Spearman’s rank correlations relate access measures to tract-level socioeconomic characteristics.

Study scope and interpretation

The indices describe potential access from census-tract centroids. Individual access also depends on insurance coverage, appointment availability, personal preferences, and ability to travel. Online ratings reflect patient satisfaction; board certification and language skills capture other dimensions of provider attributes.
